TRIP OSAKA , JAPAN

Japan’s beauty is deeply rooted in a reverence for nature, where landscapes are viewed through the aesthetic lens of yugen (ethereal mystery) and impermanence. This philosophy celebrates transient phenomena, such as the fleeting cherry blossoms (sakura) and vibrant autumn foliage, alongside majestic natural wonders like Mount Fuji, ancient cedar forests on Yakushima, and serene bamboo groves.

JAPAN

Japan’s natural beauty stretches far beyond cherry blossom season: From waterfalls to cedar forests, volcanoes, and tropical islands, the 14 spots in this blog show just how diverse Japan’s landscapes are. Many destinations are perfect for more than sightseeing, with hiking, wildlife watching, hot springs, and outdoor adventures. Nature in Japan can also be deeply cultural, with sacred shrines, pilgrimage trails, and spiritual sites woven into the scenery. Each season offers something different, from spring blossoms and summer greenery to autumn colours and snowy winter views.

SINGAPOUR

Singapore is celebrated as a lush, green city-state where 47% of its landmass is dedicated to green space, transforming it from a concrete jungle into a vibrant eco-superstar. This natural beauty is anchored by pristine environments like the Bukit Timah Nature Reserve, a rainforest established in 1883, and the iconic Gardens by the Bay, which buffers the shoreline with verdant landscapes. Founder Lee Kuan Yew intentionally integrated nature into urban planning to ensure the nation remained a livable, breathable space rather than just a business hub. Today, the country boasts four nature reserves and more than 300 parks, offering a unique blend of world-class architecture and manicured public greenery.

NEW YORK

New York City’s beauty is often described as a blend of unintentional architectural splendor and urban nature, creating a unique aesthetic that contrasts industrial grit with natural serenity. Milan Kundera characterized this as "beauty by mistake," where fortuitous forms sparkle with poetry against a backdrop of human design, while F. Scott Fitzgerald famously called the view from the Queensboro Bridge a "wild promise of all the mystery and the beauty in the world." Natural elements provide essential counterpoints to the city's density, offering pockets of tranquility such as Central Park’s green havens and the High Line’s repurposed railway gardens.
